Guides

Workout tracking guides

Practical, plain-language workout tracking guides for lifters who log their training: how to track progressive overload, what to record in a workout log, and how to plan and track the common training splits, from a three-day full body plan to PHAT and the Arnold split.

Last updated 21 October 2026

Tracking guides

  • How to track progressive overloadA simple method for tracking progressive overload with sets, reps, weight, volume and 1RM.
  • How to log your workoutsThe numbers worth recording in a gym log, and how to keep the habit going.
  • Sets per muscle group per weekWhat the research suggests for weekly sets per muscle, what counts as a set, and how to check your own week.
  • Drop sets, supersets and rest-pauseHow to do drop sets, supersets and rest-pause sets, when to use them, and how to log them properly.
  • Reading your 1RM chartWhat an estimated 1RM is, how the Epley formula works, how to read the trend, and when the number misleads.

Training split guides

  • Push Pull Legs splitThree-day and six-day Push Pull Legs layouts, what goes on each day, and how to track a PPL split.
  • Upper Lower splitTwo upper and two lower sessions a week: what goes on each day, weekly layouts, and how to track an Upper Lower split.
  • Full body workout, 3 days a weekThree full-body sessions a week: the exercises on each day, why it suits beginners, and how to track it.
  • PHUL splitThe four-day Power Hypertrophy Upper Lower split: what each day trains, who it suits, and how to run and track it.
  • PHAT splitThe five-day Power Hypertrophy Adaptive Training split: what each day trains, who it suits, and how to track it.
  • Bro splitOne body part a day, Monday to Friday: what each day trains, the trade-offs, and how to track a bro split.
  • Arnold splitChest and back, shoulders and arms, legs, each twice a week: what goes on each day and how to track the Arnold split.
  • 5/3/1 Boring But BigA three-day take on 5/3/1 Boring But Big: a main lift then 5×10 of it, how it differs from the original, and how to track it.

About these guides

The guides explain general training and logging ideas, then show how to apply them in IronBuddy, an offline workout tracker for Android. They are general information, not medical advice. If you have an injury or a health condition, speak to a qualified professional before changing your training.
