PHUL vs PHAT: four days or five of power and hypertrophy

PHUL vs PHAT is mostly a question of days. Both programs open the week with a heavy upper day and a heavy lower day, then switch to higher reps for size. PHUL does the size work in two sessions, one upper and one lower, for four days a week. PHAT splits the upper-body size work into back and shoulders on one day and chest and arms on another, for five. This guide puts the two next to each other and shows how to track whichever you choose.

Last updated 11 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Same idea: two power days early in the week, hypertrophy days after. PHUL takes four sessions, PHAT five.
  • PHAT's extra day splits upper-body hypertrophy in two, so the back, shoulders, chest and arms each get more sets.
  • PHUL is the easier week to recover from; IronBuddy lists PHUL as Intermediate and PHAT as Advanced.
  • Track both the same way: power days by weight and estimated 1RM, hypertrophy days by reps and volume.

PHUL vs PHAT at a glance

  • Training days: PHUL four, PHAT five.
  • Power days: two in both, Upper Power and Lower Power at the start of the week.
  • Hypertrophy days: PHUL two (upper, lower); PHAT three (back and shoulders, lower, chest and arms).
  • Rest days: PHUL three, PHAT two.
  • Level in IronBuddy: PHUL Intermediate, PHAT Advanced.

What the two programs share

PHUL stands for Power Hypertrophy Upper Lower; PHAT for Power Hypertrophy Adaptive Training, by Layne Norton. The first two words are the point of both: some days are for strength, with heavy weights and low reps on the compound lifts, and the rest are for size, with moderate weights, higher reps and more isolation work.

Both start the week the same way. Day one is Upper Power, day two is Lower Power, then a rest day. Every muscle is trained twice a week in both, once heavy and once for volume. Where they part is what happens after the rest day.

Where PHUL and PHAT differ

  • Days. PHUL finishes with Upper Hypertrophy and Lower Hypertrophy: four sessions. PHAT finishes with Back & Shoulders, Lower, and Chest & Arms: five.
  • Upper-body volume. On PHUL, one hypertrophy session has to cover chest, back, shoulders and arms, so each gets a few sets. On PHAT, the upper body gets two sessions, so the back and shoulders can take a dozen sets and the chest and arms another dozen. That is the reason to run PHAT: more size work for the upper body.
  • Recovery. Five sessions with two of them heavy is a lot. PHAT asks for the sleep and food to match. PHUL leaves three rest days a week and is the easier program to keep up for months.
  • Session length. PHUL's Upper Hypertrophy day is long because it carries the whole upper body. PHAT's hypertrophy days are shorter each, with more of them.
  • Who they are for. PHUL suits intermediates with four days who want both strength and size. PHAT suits intermediate and advanced lifters with five days, or a lagging upper-body part that needs its own session.

The two weeks side by side

  • PHUL: four days

    Week: Upper Power, Lower Power, rest, Upper Hypertrophy, Lower Hypertrophy, rest, rest

    Suits: Two heavy days, two higher-rep days, every muscle twice. IronBuddy's PHUL preset, set to Mon / Tue / Thu / Fri and listed as Intermediate.

  • PHAT: five days

    Week: Upper Power, Lower Power, rest, Back & Shoulders Hypertrophy, Lower Hypertrophy, Chest & Arms Hypertrophy, rest

    Suits: The same two heavy days, then three higher-rep days that split the upper body in two. IronBuddy's PHAT preset, listed as Advanced.

  • PHUL with a fifth day

    Week: Upper Power, Lower Power, rest, Upper Hypertrophy, Lower Hypertrophy, arms or a weak point, rest

    Suits: A middle path: keep PHUL and add one short day for a lagging body part. In IronBuddy, edit the PHUL preset and add a day; your copy goes under My splits.

Which one to pick

  • Four days: PHUL. It is not a cut-down PHAT; it is a complete program at four days, and the version most people can recover from.
  • Five days and a year of training: PHAT. The fifth day only pays off if you recover from it. If your Friday and Saturday numbers keep falling, it is too much for now.
  • A lagging upper-body part: PHAT, or PHUL plus a day. Either gives that area its own session. The PHUL-plus-a-day layout above is the lighter way in.
  • Learning the lifts: neither yet. Run a three-day full body plan or an Upper Lower split first.

How to track PHUL or PHAT

  • Two kinds of day, two numbers. On the power days, watch the weight on the main lifts and the estimated 1RM chart. On the hypertrophy days, watch reps and session volume. The week is working when both climb.
  • Each day against itself. Upper Power against last week's Upper Power, Chest & Arms against last Chest & Arms. The same exercise can appear on a power day and a hypertrophy day, so compare per day. IronBuddy's workout summary compares every exercise with its previous session, which on these programs is the same day a week ago.
  • Weight on power days, reps on hypertrophy days. When every power set hits the top of its range, add weight. When every hypertrophy set hits the top, add weight and drop back to the bottom of the range. The progressive overload guide covers both.
  • Weekly sets per muscle. PHAT spreads chest and back over two days each. Muscle Balance on IronBuddy's Home adds the sets up by body area, so you can see whether the extra day is landing where you wanted it.

PHUL vs PHAT questions

PHUL or PHAT: which should I run?

Count your training days. With four, run PHUL; it covers strength and size for every muscle twice a week. With five, and a year or so of training behind you, PHAT uses the extra day to split the upper-body hypertrophy work in two. If you are between the two, run PHUL first: it is the easier week to recover from, and PHAT is the same idea with one day more.

Is PHUL or PHAT better for beginners?

Neither is a beginner plan, but PHUL is the closer of the two. IronBuddy lists PHUL as Intermediate and PHAT as Advanced. A beginner gets more from a three-day full body plan or an Upper Lower split, then moves to PHUL once the lifts are learned and four sessions a week feel normal.

Is PHUL or PHAT better for size?

PHAT gives the upper body more hypertrophy work, because its extra day splits back and shoulders from chest and arms. That only helps if you recover from five sessions. If the extra day leaves your power days weaker each week, PHUL with its three rest days will build more over months.

Is PHUL or PHAT better for strength?

Neither has an edge on paper: both open the week with the same two heavy days, Upper Power and Lower Power. The difference is recovery. PHUL leaves more rest around the heavy days, which many lifters find makes it easier to keep adding weight.

What about PHUL vs PHAT vs PPL?

Push Pull Legs is a different kind of split: it groups by movement and has no separate power and hypertrophy days. Six-day PPL trains every muscle twice, like PHUL and PHAT, but every session is the same style. Pick PHUL or PHAT when you want heavy low-rep days and lighter high-rep days in the same week; pick PPL when you want every session to look alike and be easy to repeat.

Can I move from PHUL to PHAT later?

Yes, and it is the natural order. The two power days are the same idea in both programs, so your heavy lifts carry over. In IronBuddy, set the PHAT preset active when you are ready; your exercise history stays with each exercise, so the summary still compares the heavy lifts with last time.

A note on safety

This guide is general training information, not medical advice. Choose loads and a weekly layout that suit your experience and recovery, and speak to a qualified professional if you have an injury or a health condition.
